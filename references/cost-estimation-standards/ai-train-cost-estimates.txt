Core Data and Context Setup 

Historical data: Upload past project budgets, actual final costs, and variances to teach the AI baseline pricing.

Rate libraries: Provide standard material, labor, and productivity rates via files or connected databases (like Excel or Airtable).

Scope documents: Supply the project requirements, blueprints, or technical specifications so the AI knows what the estimate should cover.


Defining System Instructions

Role assignment: Instruct the AI to act as a senior cost auditor or professional estimator.

Evaluation criteria: Tell it to check for mathematical accuracy, market rate alignment, and omitted line items.

Output format: Require structured feedback, such as a variance report, a list of red flags, or a Request for Information (RFI) log


Iterative Testing and Refinement

Test past audits: Run old, known estimates through the AI to see if it catches the historical errors.

Tune assumptions: Refine the prompt instructions when the AI accepts unworkable assumptions or misses hidden project risks.

Keep a human expert in the loop to verify the AI's flagged discrepancies before making financial decisions.


To teach a custom model like GenAI.mil to evaluate military system cost estimates, you must train it to cross-reference technical specifications against rigid defense acquisition frameworks. Defense estimates depend heavily on specialized manufacturing, security compliance, and long-term sustainment rather than standard market rates.

Here is the step-by-step implementation blueprint to train your custom model.

Step 1: Ingest Defense Reference Frameworks 

Military cost estimation relies on standardized structures. Your model must use these as its baseline evaluation truth.

WBS alignment: Ingest DoD MIL-STD-881F (Work Breakdown Structures)(https://quicksearch.dla.mil/) to teach the AI standard defense system hierarchies (e.g., air vehicle, training, data).

Cost categories: Train the model on the four primary defense life-cycle categories: Research & Development (RDT&E), Procurement, Operations & Support (O&S), and Disposal.

CARD data: Provide historical Cost Analysis Requirements Description (CARD) documents so the AI understands the precise technical and programmatic baseline of legacy systems.

Step 2: Feed Historical Cost Databases

Custom GenAI models evaluate estimates by comparing new proposals against historical anchor points.

CADE integration: Connect the model to past Cost Assessment Data Enterprise (CADE) records to train it on historical actual costs.

Unclassified SARs: Seed the training data with historical Selected Acquisition Reports (SARs) to teach the AI how systemic risks historically drive cost growth.

Inflation indices: Load the Office of the Secretary of Defense (OSD) Inflation Rates to ensure the AI converts past fiscal year dollars to current-year values accurately.

Step 3: Train for Military Risk Factors

Unlike commercial software or manufacturing, military systems have hidden cost drivers that general AI models miss. Teach your model to flag these specific anomalies:

TRL mismatches: Instruct the AI to flag low Technology Readiness Levels (TRL < 6) that feature unrealistically low engineering cost estimates.

Security & compliance: Train the AI to audit for missing line items related to Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification (CMMC) and specialized military testing (DT&E/OT&E).

Supply chain single points: Teach the AI to scan Bill of Materials (BOM) data for components that violate National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) sourcing restrictions.

Military programs are highly vulnerable to optimistic bias and early-stage cost underestimation.

Learning curve application: Program the model to check if manufacturing labor hours follow a standard defense learning curve formula (e.g., \(Y = aX^b\)) as production volume increases.

2. Step-by-Step Training Logic & Guardrails

Step A: Structural Compliance (The FAR & WBS Check)The AI must verify that the contractor is billing transparently and mapping costs to the correct buckets.

WBS Integrity: Teach the model to check if the proposal maps perfectly to the requested Work Breakdown Structure (MIL-STD-881F). If the contractor lumps "System Engineering" and "Data" into a single unmapped line item, the AI must flag it.

FAR/DFARS Compliance: Train the model to look for statements of compliance regarding FAR Part 15 (Contract Pricing). It must explicitly scan for certified cost or pricing data if the proposal exceeds the current statutory TINA (Truth in Negotiations Act) threshold.

Step B: Rate Realism & Parametrics (The Sanity Check)Contractors often pad labor rates or overestimate the hours required for complex defense engineering.

Labor Category Mapping: Connect GenAI.mil to a reference database of standard defense labor rates (e.g., DCAA-approved forward pricing rate agreements or federal labor categories like GS scales / contractor equivalents). The AI must flag any engineering or project management labor hour rates that sit >15% above the historical median for that geographic region.

The Learning Curve Audit: For production proposals, contractors apply a "learning curve" to show that successive units get cheaper to build. Instruct the AI to mathematically check the contractor's stated learning curve. If the contractor assumes an overly aggressive 70% learning curve without historical factory proof, or conversely, a flat 100% curve (claiming no efficiency gains), the AI must trigger a variance alert.

Step C: Risk and Omission Auditing (The "Hidden Cost" Check)Contractors sometimes underbid a proposal by deliberately leaving out expensive, mandatory defense requirements to win the award, intending to claw back money later via engineering change proposals (ECPs). Train the AI to actively scan for what is missing:

Sustainment & Data Rights: Does the proposal omit Technical Data Rights fees? Does it omit long-term depot maintenance planning or interactive electronic technical manuals (IETMs)?

Cybersecurity Compliance: Does the proposal lack dedicated budget allocations for continuous CMMC monitoring and cryptographic hardware compliance?

Testing Support: Ensure the contractor has adequately budgeted for their portion of Developmental Testing (DT) and Operational Testing (OT) support, including range time and test articles.
